# ROBLOX EMPIRE PLAN — Full Audit: 2006→2026 History, 18 Concepts, Debate, Verdict
*Compiled 2026-09-21. Research: ~30 targeted passes across 20 years of Roblox history (era hits, fall-offs, monetization data, retention benchmarks, algorithm shifts). Not "thousands of data looks" — that's not a real thing anyone can do by hand; this is deep cross-era pattern synthesis instead, and it's honest about it.*

---

# PART 1 — THE FULL HISTORY: 2006 → 2026

## Era 1: 2006–2012 — The Sandbox Years (simplicity + social physics)
**Defining hits:** Rocket Arena (2006), Crossroads (2006, Shedletsky — Roblox's first true multiplayer game), Sword Fights on the Heights IV (2007), Base Wars FPS (2007 — first class-based shooter on the platform, ancestor of every Roblox FPS), Chaos Canyon, Natural Disaster Survival (2011, Stickmasterluke), Work at a Pizza Place (2008, Dued1), Survive the Disasters (2011).
**What they did right:** Dead-simple loops a 9-year-old understood in 10 seconds. Physics were the content — rocket-jumping, collapsing towers, flinging. Everything was social by default; servers were playgrounds, not matchmaking queues. Zero tutorial needed.
**Graveyard:** Crossroads declined from 2014, taken down ~2017 (FilteringEnabled migration broke old code), later revived as a museum piece. Chaos Canyon unplayable since 2017. **Cause of death:** platform tech moved on; games were tech demos, not live services. **Law #1: tech demos die; systems live.**

## Era 2: 2013–2016 — Tycoons, Roleplay & the First Professionals
**Defining hits:** Murder Mystery 2 (2014), Welcome to Bloxburg (2014, paid access — proved players pay upfront), Phantom Forces (2015), MeepCity (2016 — first game to 1 BILLION visits), Pokémon Brick Bronze (2016).
**What they did right:** MM2 = 3-minute social-deduction rounds, infinite replayability, tradable knives (player economy). Bloxburg = persistent identity + building. MeepCity = Club Penguin formula + house parties + minigames; pure hangout gravity.
**Graveyard:**
- **MeepCity** — the most instructive death in Roblox history. Parties + custom avatars/bios became an underage-dating vector ("ODing"). Roblox forced party removal, then in 2025 forced removal of bios/custom avatars → **-80% players overnight**, now a few hundred CCU. *Cause: the social feature WAS the product; unmoderated UGC social is a time bomb.*
- **Pokémon Brick Bronze** — DMCA'd by Nintendo (2018). *Cause: borrowed IP = borrowed time. The single biggest avoidable killer.*
- **Speed Run 4 / generic obbies** — out-competed by Tower of Hell's innovation. *Cause: no moat on a simple loop.*
**Law #2: never build on someone else's IP. Law #3: if your core feature can't survive moderation, you don't have a game.**

## Era 3: 2017–2019 — The First Mega-Hits & the Money Era
**Defining hits:** Jailbreak (2017, Badimo — cops vs robbers open world; first modern mega-hit, made millions), Royale High (2018), Tower of Hell (2018 — obby reinvented as brutal RNG tower, no checkpoints), Arsenal (2019), Mad City (2019).
**What they did right:** Jailbreak = GTA-for-kids fantasy + heists + cars + seasons; proved a 2-person team could print money. Tower of Hell = 8-minute rounds, zero downtime, pure skill flex (clip-bait before TikTok existed). Royale High = identity/fashion as gameplay.
**Graveyard:**
- **Jailbreak** — slow decline from ~2021. *Cause: update cadence collapsed (Badimo went quiet for long stretches), loop aged, competitors iterated. The #1 killer of era-3 giants: content drought.*
- **Royale High** — audience aged out, updates slowed, fashion crown taken by Dress to Impress. *Cause: demographic treadmill — your players grow up; if you don't recruit younger cohorts, you die of old age.*
- **Mad City** — labeled a Jailbreak clone, never escaped the shadow. *Cause: being #2 in someone else's genre is death.*
**Law #4: update cadence is oxygen. Law #5: clones die; the original's community forgives droughts, the clone's doesn't.**

## Era 4: 2020–2022 — The Pandemic Boom (horror + pets + RP)
**Defining hits:** Adopt Me! (peak 1.9M CCU April 2021; held "most visited" 1,197 days straight), Piggy (2020 — story-horror phenomenon, ~400k CCU, 13B visits), Brookhaven RP (2020 — now 76.4 BILLION visits, the most-visited game ever), BedWars (2021), Pet Simulator X (2021, 1.47M CCU), Doors (2022 — hotel horror, still alive), Rainbow Friends (2022), Deepwoken (2022 — hardcore permadeath RPG, niche-immortal), 3008 (SCP-IKEA horror, 2.2B visits), Evade (2022).
**What they did right:** Piggy = episodic story chapters (appointment gaming — players returned for each chapter like TV episodes) + theory-crafting community. Adopt Me = trading economy (player-to-player economy = infinite content you don't have to build) + pets as status. Brookhaven = zero objectives, pure social sandbox; proved "no game" can beat every game. Doors = 100-room runs, entities as memes (Figure, Seek), co-op terror.
**Graveyard:**
- **Piggy** — story ended, hype cooled, then **Piggy: Intercity** took 5 years and tens of thousands of dollars and launched to a **99% player collapse (<1,000 CCU within weeks)**. *Cause: the 5-year dev cycle missed the zeitgeist; hype is perishable; sequels to phenomena almost never work (the audience was 10 in 2020 and 15 in 2025).*
- **Pet Simulator X** — BIG Games pivoted to Pet Sim 99; broken promises, sequel cannibalized the original. *Cause: sequel-cannibalism + overpromising.*
- **Rainbow Friends** — faded as Poppy Playtime inspiration cooled and updates slowed. *Cause: trend-riding without a live-ops engine.*
- **Adopt Me** — fell off #1 but SURVIVED via relentless pet/event live-ops. The counterexample: live-ops beats gravity.
**Law #6: hype is perishable — ship in months, not years. Law #7: episodic content is appointment gaming; it prints retention. Law #8: player economies (trading) are infinite free content.**

## Era 5: 2023–2024 — Memes, Anime & the Skill-Game Resurgence
**Defining hits:** Toilet Tower Defense (2023 — Skibidi meme TD), The Strongest Battlegrounds (2023, 1.4M CCU — anime battler, combo-heavy PvP), Blade Ball (2023), Dress to Impress (Nov 2023 — 1B visits by June 2024, 1.74M CCU; fashion-voting), Pet Simulator 99 (2023), Blue Lock: Rivals (2024, 1.1M CCU), Rivals (2024, 560k CCU — pure gun-skill shooter), Fisch (late 2024, 1.27M CCU — cozy fishing incremental), Dandy's World (2024, 867k CCU — mascot horror), Forsaken (2024, 1.2M CCU — asymmetric horror), Dead Rails (early 2025, 1.48M CCU — zombie train survival), a dusty trip (2024, 573k CCU — road-trip survival), Pressure (2023 — Doors-inspired hardcore horror).
**What they did right:** TSB = combat feel (hitstop, VFX) as the entire product; proved *game feel* beats content volume. DTI = voting/judging loop (players ARE the content) + 5-minute rounds + TikTok-native. Fisch = cozy incremental + collection + offline-ish patience; the Grow-a-Garden prototype. Rivals = ranked skill expression; proved older players (13+) want competitive depth. Dead Rails = co-op survival + train base-building + high lethality = clip factory.
**Graveyard:**
- **Toilet Tower Defense** — meme died (Skibidi cycle ~18 months), copyright pressure. *Cause: meme games have the half-life of the meme. You must convert meme traffic into systems before the meme dies.*
- **TSB** — "is dying" discourse from update droughts + exploit/cheating scandals (leaderboard kill-farming). *Cause: competitive games die twice — once from drought, once from cheaters.*
- **Blade Ball clones** — dozens of "ball deflection" copies; only the original kept gravity. *Law #5 again.*
**Law #9: meme traffic is rented, not owned — convert it to systems in <6 months. Law #10: competitive integrity is existential; one cheat wave can end you.**

## Era 6: 2025–2026 — The Scale Singularity & the Retention Pivot
**Defining hits:** Grow a Garden (March 2025 — 22.4M CCU peak, 35B+ visits; made by a teenager in days, scaled by Splitting Point + DoBig live-ops), Steal a Brainrot (May 2025 — **25.8M CCU**, the all-time record; Italian-brainrot collect-and-steal tycoon), 99 Nights in the Forest (March 2025 — 14.2M CCU peak; co-op survival horror; now getting a Sam Raimi movie), Plants vs Brainrots (8.6M CCU), Fish It (2.7M CCU), The Forge (1M CCU), Ink Game (810k CCU — Squid Game), Find the Brainrot (1M CCU).
**What they did right:** Grow a Garden = offline progression (garden grows while you're gone → daily check-in compulsion) + cozy + pets/mutations + relentless events; proved calm beats chaos. Steal a Brainrot = steal-from-each-other PvP inside a tycoon (social conflict = infinite drama = infinite clips) + brainrot meme fuel. 99 Nights = day/night survival + co-op roles + weekly updates; proved depth + teamwork beats passive loops for older players. Common thread: **all three are simple to start, social by default, and run aggressive live-ops.**
**The 2026 slump (critical context):** DAU fell 152M → 123M in early 2026; Roblox's stock -70%. Roblox's own shareholder letter admits: engagement shifted from "high-monetizing 2025-vintage viral games" to lower-monetizing games, and the **"Recommended For You" algorithm now intentionally boosts highly-retentive games at the expense of near-term monetization** — the retention window widened to **28 days**, and **intentional co-play is a weighted signal**. Translation: **in 2026, the algorithm pays you for D7/D28 retention and friends playing together, not for squeezing purchases.**
**The money math (2025-2026, official):** DevEx $0.0038/Robux ($0.0054 for US 18+ verified). $1.5B paid to creators in 2025. Top 1,000 devs avg ~$980k–$1.3M/yr. **Median dev: ~$1,440–$1,500/yr. 85% earn <$100/month. 74% of devs are solo.** GameAnalytics (500+ titles, 1M+ MAU): median **D1 10.3%, D7 1.6%, D30 0.5%** — that's the real baseline; "good D1 = 20%" charts are survivorship bias from already-promoted games. Hit territory = 19–24 min sessions, ~2 sessions/day. Top 1% of payers spend $86+; spending concentrates brutally. Audience aging: 54% YoY growth in 13+ players; ~60% teen/adult in 2025.

## THE 10 LAWS (distilled from 20 years of corpses and crowns)
1. Tech demos die; systems live.
2. Never build on someone else's IP (DMCA is a guillotine).
3. If your core feature can't survive moderation, you don't have a game.
4. Update cadence is oxygen — weekly/biweekly or decay.
5. Clones die; #2 in someone else's genre is death.
6. Hype is perishable — ship in months, not years (Piggy: Intercity = the $50k tombstone).
7. Episodic/appointment content prints retention.
8. Player economies and player-judged content are infinite free content.
9. Meme traffic is rented — convert to systems within 6 months.
10. **2026 meta: the algorithm now pays for 28-day retention + co-play. Build for return visits and friends-together, monetize second.**

# PART 2 — THE 18 CONCEPTS

*Format per concept: 30-second core loop → 60-second hook → retention (D1/D7/D30) → monetization → social/viral → player-base estimate (peak CCU if it lands) → build complexity. Estimates are honest: base rate for any Roblox game is <1k CCU; "if it lands" numbers are calibrated to genre precedents.*

---

### 1. DEAD DROP DISTRICT
**Core loop (30s):** Grab a contract, pick your route through a neon city, sprint/parkour past rival crews, stash the package in the drop zone, get paid, buy better gear. Risk/reward: bigger payouts cross hotter zones.
**60-sec hook:** You spawn, a glowing package lands in your hands, a 90-second timer starts, and three rival players are already hunting you. Instant chase.
**Retention:** D1 — rank ladder + daily contracts. D7 — crew (guild) turf wars over districts. D30 — seasonal "heat" resets with exclusive courier skins.
**Monetization:** Sprint/parkour gear gamepasses, custom drop-trails, crew emblems, contract refreshes (developer products).
**Social/viral:** Hunter-vs-courier asymmetry is clip fuel (chase fails); crews recruit friends for turf wars.
**Player base if it lands:** 300k–800k CCU (extraction-chase lane; comps: Jailbreak, Dead Rails).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 5–7 months) — city map, AI hunters + PvP, parkour feel.

### 2. STARFORGE SALVAGE
**Core loop (30s):** With 3 friends, tether into a derelict ship, cut it apart with plasma torches in zero-G, haul scrap to the rig while the reactor meltdown timer ticks.
**60-sec hook:** First 60 seconds: you're floating inside a dead capital ship, your torch ignites, metal peels away, alarms start. No tutorial — the ship teaches you.
**Retention:** D1 — ship classes + tool upgrades. D7 — weekly "derelict rotation" (new ships, new hazards). D30 — salvage company ranks, legendary wreck events.
**Monetization:** Torch/ship skins, suit cosmetics, salvage-boost passes, extra tether slots.
**Social/viral:** Co-op chaos (someone cuts the wrong beam and vents the room) = Shorts gold; requires 2–4 players = co-play signal the algorithm now rewards.
**Player base if it lands:** 500k–1.5M CCU (co-op survival lane; comps: 99 Nights, Dead Rails).
**Build complexity:** Small team → studio (4–6, 7–10 months) — zero-G physics, destructible ships, netcode.

### 3. GRIMOIRE ACADEMY
**Core loop (30s):** Combine discovered runes into custom spells (fire + spread + bounce = your signature), dive co-op dungeons, loot new runes.
**60-sec hook:** You pick up 3 runes in the first minute and immediately invent a spell no one else has. Creation, not selection.
**Retention:** D1 — spell-crafting experimentation + dorm social hub. D7 — weekly dungeon wings, house (guild) competitions. D30 — ranked dueling ladder, semester resets with legacy rewards.
**Monetization:** Rune pack gacha (careful: no pay-to-win spells — cosmetics + convenience), wand/staff skins, dorm decorations, extra spell slots.
**Social/viral:** "Look at this cursed spell I made" is inherently shareable; dueling clips.
**Player base if it lands:** 400k–1M CCU (magic-school fantasy; comps: Arcane Odyssey, Blox Fruits audience).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–5, 6–9 months) — combinatorial spell system, dungeon generation, balance.

### 4. NEON NIGHT MARKET
**Core loop (30s):** Run your food stall: cook dishes in rhythm/timing minigames, serve the rush, haggle with customers, reinvest in your stall.
**60-sec hook:** The night bell rings, customers flood in, your first dish sizzles — you're cooking within 20 seconds, money cha-chings immediately.
**Retention:** D1 — recipe unlocks + stall upgrades. D7 — night-market reputation ranks, weekend festival events. D30 — franchise (multiple stalls), seasonal menus.
**Monetization:** Stall cosmetics, chef outfits, recipe packs, rush-hour boosts, premium ingredients.
**Social/viral:** Friends run adjacent stalls (co-play); food-review roleplay; cozy clips do numbers on TikTok.
**Player base if it lands:** 200k–600k CCU (cozy sim lane; comps: Grow a Garden, Restaurant Tycoon 2 lineage).
**Build complexity:** Solo–small team (1–3, 3–5 months) — minigames, economy, low technical risk.

### 5. THE LAST LIGHTHOUSE
**Core loop (30s):** Asymmetric 1v7 — one Keeper (the dark) hunts; seven survivors gather fuel to keep the lighthouse burning. Light is ammo, safety, and objective.
**60-sec hook:** Lights die 60 seconds in, the Keeper's eyes open in the dark, and your flashlight is the only thing between you and it. Instant dread.
**Retention:** D1 — survivor perks + Keeper powers unlock. D7 — map rotation, weekly "storm nights." D30 — ranked Keeper ladder, survivor prestige.
**Monetization:** Keeper skins/powers (cosmetic-adjacent), survivor outfits, light-trail effects, private lobbies.
**Social/viral:** Asymmetric horror = screaming friends = the 99 Nights/Forsaken clip formula; 1v7 forces friend groups.
**Player base if it lands:** 400k–1.2M CCU (asymmetric horror lane; comps: Forsaken, Doors).
**Build complexity:** Small team (2–4, 4–6 months) — light/darkness systems, one strong monster kit, 2–3 maps.

### 6. MYCELIUM
**Core loop (30s):** You ARE a fungal network spreading under a town — extend hyphae, digest resources, convert buildings; 1–2 exterminator players hunt your core.
**60-sec hook:** You start as a single spore, pulse outward, and watch the minimap bloom with your color. Playing as the environment is instantly novel.
**Retention:** D1 — strain mutations (build variety). D7 — town layouts rotate, exterminator tech tree. D30 — "bloom events," strain leaderboards.
**Monetization:** Strain skins, spore-trail effects, mutation slots, exterminator gear.
**Social/viral:** Novelty ("you play as fungus") earns clicks; underground-vs-surface asymmetry clips.
**Player base if it lands:** 50k–250k CCU (novelty strategy; high variance).
**Build complexity:** Small team (2–3, 5–7 months) — network-growth sim, fog-of-war, balance is hard.

### 7. BLOCKBUSTER HEIST CREW
**Core loop (30s):** 4-player crew, assigned roles (hacker, muscle, driver, lookout): case the joint, breach, grab, escape while Heat rises.
**60-sec hook:** The van door slides open, your crew chief marks the target, 3 minutes on the clock. Ocean's Eleven in 60 seconds.
**Retention:** D1 — role unlocks + crew XP. D7 — weekly new targets (bank, museum, casino). D30 — Heat seasons, legendary heists, crew rivalries.
**Monetization:** Role gear, masks/outfits, getaway vehicle skins, heist-plan slots, private crew servers.
**Social/viral:** Requires 4 = built-in friend recruitment; heist-gone-wrong clips are evergreen.
**Player base if it lands:** 300k–900k CCU (co-op heist lane; comps: Jailbreak heists, Entry Point lineage).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–5, 6–9 months) — AI guards, role systems, multiple heist maps.

### 8. TIDEBOUND
**Core loop (30s):** Build and expand a raft with your crew, sail open seas, fish/dive for resources, survive storms and leviathans, trade with passing ships.
**60-sec hook:** You wake on a 4-plank raft, a storm wall is coming, and your crew is yelling to build. Survival urgency + building freedom instantly.
**Retention:** D1 — raft expansion + crafting. D7 — island/leviathan rotation, crew alliances. D30 — fleet ranks, seasonal seas.
**Monetization:** Sail/raft cosmetics, figureheads, pet seabirds, resource boosts, private seas.
**Social/viral:** Crew-built rafts = pride + screenshots; leviathan attacks = clips; Raft proved the fantasy.
**Player base if it lands:** 300k–800k CCU (co-op survival; comps: 99 Nights, Dead Rails).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 6–8 months) — water physics, building system, sea creatures.

### 9. ECHOES OF THE DEEP
**Core loop (30s):** Descend a flooded facility with 3 friends; manage oxygen and light; the creature learns your routes and ambushes accordingly.
**60-sec hook:** Elevator floods behind you in the first minute — no way back up. Dread + commitment instantly.
**Retention:** D1 — depth unlocks + gear. D7 — facility wings rotate, "the creature adapts" (new behaviors). D30 — depth leaderboards, prestige dives.
**Monetization:** Diving suit cosmetics, light colors, sonar upgrades, revive tokens (careful with difficulty-selling).
**Social/viral:** Adaptive-AI horror stories ("it KNEW we'd go left") are uniquely shareable.
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 6–9 months) — adaptive AI director, underwater systems, hard to tune.

### 10. POCKET PANTHEON
**Core loop (30s):** You're a god. Tiny worshippers pray; answer prayers (tap to bless), smite problems, earn Faith while offline; spend Faith to grow your civilization.
**60-sec hook:** First prayer pops in 15 seconds — you tap it, lightning blesses a harvest, your worshippers cheer. God-feeling in under a minute.
**Retention:** D1 — miracle unlocks + temple building. D7 — daily prayer rituals, god-vs-god blessing raids, weekly "omens" (events). D30 — pantheon ages (resets with permanent relics), civilization eras.
**Monetization:** Divine cosmetics (lightning colors, temple styles), miracle cooldown skips, worshipper skins, faith boosts — idle-game monetization is the highest-converting genre on Roblox.
**Social/viral:** Visit friends' pantheons, bless/curse each other; temple builds = flex screenshots. Weaker on raw clips — compensated by retention.
**Player base if it lands:** 500k–2M CCU (idle lane; comps: Grow a Garden's offline loop + god-game novelty).
**Build complexity:** SOLO-friendly (1–2 devs, 3–5 months) — mostly simulation + UI; lowest technical risk on this list.

### 11. SCRAPYARD RACERS
**Core loop (30s):** Build a kart from junk parts (engine + wheels + armor + weapon), race 8-player chaotic tracks, wreck or be wrecked.
**60-sec hook:** You bolt together a kart in 45 seconds and you're racing. Building IS the onboarding.
**Retention:** D1 — part unlocks + track variety. D7 — weekly track rotation, ranked leagues. D30 — build-meta seasons, tournament events.
**Monetization:** Part cosmetics, paint/wraps, horn sounds, victory animations, garage slots.
**Social/viral:** Cursed builds ("shopping cart with a V8") are meme gold; party racing with friends.
**Player base if it lands:** 200k–700k CCU (party racer lane; comps: Mario Kart fantasy, Jailbreak driving).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 6–8 months) — vehicle physics on Roblox is genuinely painful; highest-feel-risk item here.

### 12. THE MIMIC MARKET
**Core loop (30s):** Run a market stall trading goods with players; 1–2 shapeshifters per server sell counterfeits — inspect, accuse, vote them out.
**60-sec hook:** First trade in 60 seconds; someone immediately accuses YOU. Paranoia from minute one.
**Retention:** D1 — trader reputation + appraisal skill. D7 — market events, rare-goods drops. D30 — merchant guilds, season economies.
**Monetization:** Stall cosmetics, appraisal tools, disguise kits (for fun modes), emotes.
**Social/viral:** Social deduction = Among Us-proven clip factory; player-driven economy = free content (Law #8).
**Player base if it lands:** 150k–500k CCU (social deduction lane; comps: MM2's round structure).
**Build complexity:** Small team (2–3, 4–6 months) — trading systems, deduction mechanics; exploit-proofing trades is the hard part.

### 13. ASTRAL ARCHITECTS
**Core loop (30s):** Co-op colony on a living planet that reshapes itself — raise domes, route resources, adapt when the planet "dreams" (terrain events).
**60-sec hook:** The ground breathes under you in the first minute. The planet is a character, not a map.
**Retention:** D1 — tech tree + colony roles. D7 — planet cycles, co-op megaprojects. D30 — colony eras, cross-server wonders.
**Monetization:** Dome/building cosmetics, colonist outfits, planet-skins, boost passes.
**Social/viral:** Megaproject screenshots; weaker clip game — it's a slow-burn builder.
**Player base if it lands:** 100k–400k CCU (colony sim; niche but loyal).
**Build complexity:** Studio (4–6, 8–12 months) — living-world systems, heavy sim; worst feasibility on the list.

### 14. KAIJU KITCHEN
**Core loop (30s):** Run a restaurant where the customers are 300-foot kaiju — giant cooking minigames (flip a building-sized pancake), serve, dodge stomps.
**60-sec hook:** A kaiju's eye fills your window 30 seconds in and it honks for food. Absurdity = instant delight.
**Retention:** D1 — recipe unlocks + kitchen upgrades. D7 — kaiju regulars (relationship system), weekly food festivals. D30 — restaurant chains, kaiju-chef prestige.
**Monetization:** Kitchen cosmetics, chef outfits, kaiju-food plating styles, rush boosts.
**Social/viral:** Absurdist giant-cooking clips are elite Shorts bait; "cozy chaos" is an untapped lane.
**Player base if it lands:** 200k–700k CCU (cozy-chaos; comps: Grow a Garden's calm + meme energy).
**Build complexity:** Small team (2–3, 4–6 months) — kaiju animation, minigames; moderate.

### 15. BLACKOUT PROTOCOL
**Core loop (30s):** 6-player co-op: keep a facility's generators alive in rolling blackouts; an entity hunts in the dark; roles (engineer, medic, scout).
**60-sec hook:** Lights die at 0:45, the entity's breathing starts, your engineer is screaming for a fuse. Immediate co-op panic.
**Retention:** D1 — role unlocks + facility wings. D7 — blackout modifiers, weekly entity variants. D30 — prestige roles, hardcore mode.
**Monetization:** Role cosmetics, flashlight effects, suit skins, private facility servers.
**Social/viral:** 6-player panic = clip factory; role-based play recruits friend groups.
**Player base if it lands:** 300k–900k CCU (co-op horror lane; comps: Doors, 99 Nights).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 5–7 months) — darkness systems, entity AI, facility generation.

### 16. REWIND RAIDERS
**Core loop (30s):** 4-player heists inside a 60-second time loop — each loop you keep your knowledge; layer plans across loops (loop 1: scout, loop 2: disable, loop 3: grab).
**60-sec hook:** First loop ends at 0:60 — time snaps back, but YOUR ghost replays what you just did. You watch yourself. Mind blown in one minute.
**Retention:** D1 — loop-ghost tactics + target variety. D7 — weekly vaults (new layouts), crew loop-records. D30 — "perfect heist" leaderboards, paradox seasons.
**Monetization:** Ghost-trail effects, crew outfits, vault themes, loop-planner tools, private vaults.
**Social/viral:** Ghost-replay clips ("watch our 5-loop plan execute") are a NEW clip format — novelty is the algorithm's favorite food.
**Player base if it lands:** 400k–1.2M CCU (heist lane with a twist; comps: Entry Point, Jailbreak).
**Build complexity:** Small team (3–4, 6–9 months) — deterministic ghost replay is technically spicy; desync is the dragon.

### 17. THE UNDERSTUDY
**Core loop (30s):** Improv theater party game — audience votes prompts, players act scenes with emotes/props, audience judges.
**60-sec hook:** You're on stage in 30 seconds with a prompt like "a pirate apologizing to his parrot." Instant comedy.
**Retention:** D1 — prop/emote unlocks. D7 — themed nights, troupe (guild) rivalries. D30 — season championships.
**Monetization:** Costumes, props, stage themes, emote packs — Roblox players spend absurdly on emotes (Roblox's own 2025 data: emotes reshaped Marketplace search; "dance" topped queries).
**Social/viral:** Player performances ARE the content (Law #8); improv clips travel.
**Player base if it lands:** 100k–400k CCU (party game; high variance).
**Build complexity:** SOLO-friendly (1–2, 2–4 months) — stage, prompts, voting; trivially buildable.
**Fatal flaw preview:** No progression depth — party games on Roblox (excluding Epic Minigames' minigame buffet) struggle to hold D30. Fun ≠ retention.

### 18. DRIFTWOOD DYNASTY
**Core loop (30s):** Build a barge empire on a procedural river — trade between towns, dodge pirates, set offline trade routes that earn while you're gone.
**60-sec hook:** You push off the dock in 30 seconds, cargo loaded, first pirate sail on the horizon. Tycoon + adventure instantly.
**Retention:** D1 — barge upgrades + route unlocks. D7 — river events, trading-company (guild) competition. D30 — dynasty prestige (generational resets with heirlooms), seasonal rivers.
**Monetization:** Barge cosmetics, figureheads, crew outfits, route slots, trade boosts — tycoon monetization is proven (Grow a Garden's entire economy).
**Social/viral:** Trading companies recruit friends; river screenshots; weaker on clips, strong on retention.
**Player base if it lands:** 400k–1.5M CCU (tycoon/idle lane; comps: Grow a Garden's offline formula applied to trading).
**Build complexity:** Small team (2–3, 4–6 months) — economy sim + procedural river; very buildable.

# PART 3 — THE DEBATE: BULL vs BEAR, SCORED

*Scoring 1–10 on six axes: Hook strength | Retention | Monetization | Virality | Build feasibility | Competition moat. Scored against the 2026 reality: algorithm rewards 28-day retention + co-play; median D1 is 10.3%; 74% of devs are solo. Brutal honesty enforced — weak ideas get killed with numbers.*

---

### 1. DEAD DROP DISTRICT — 42
**BULL:** Extraction is the hottest loop in wider gaming (Escape from Tarkov, Hunt) and Roblox has no real extraction game. Chase gameplay is instant clip fuel; crew turf wars give D30 meaning. Monetizes like a battler (gear, trails).

## 🏆 POCKET PANTHEON

**Why it survives when the other four don't:**

1. **It is the only top-5 idea a solo developer can actually ship.** Rewind Raiders might not survive its own netcode. Starforge Salvage needs a team and 10 months. Lighthouse and Driftwood need small teams. Pantheon ships in 3–5 months with 1–2 people. An idea that can't be built is a fantasy, not a plan — and the graveyard is full of fantasies (Piggy: Intercity, Astral Architects).

2. **It is purpose-built for the 2026 algorithm.** The retention window is 28 days; co-play is weighted; monetization is secondary to return visits. Offline Faith + daily prayer rituals + weekly omens + god-raids + pantheon ages is a D1/D7/D30 architecture, not a hope. Every other finalist has a retention story with a hole in it (Raiders: comprehension bounce; Salvage: content treadmill; Lighthouse: queueing; Driftwood: incumbent collision). Pantheon's hole — weak virality — is the least fatal hole, because the algorithm now pays for retention over virality.

3. **The genre math is undeniable.** Idle/cozy is the biggest lane on the platform (Grow a Garden 22.4M CCU, Steal a Brainrot 25.8M CCU both run idle-collection cores). Idle monetization converts best. And god-games are a COMPLETE whitespace on Roblox — the fantasy has never been done, which means no incumbent, no clone-shadow, no 'worse Doors' problem.

4. **It has the cheapest corpse.** If it fails, it fails in month 5 having cost one developer's time — not month 10 with a team's runway burned. Expected value = probability × payoff ÷ cost. Pantheon wins on probability AND cost, which more than covers its lower virality ceiling.

5. **The bear case was answered structurally, not hopefully.** Visit-able pantheons + bless/curse drama = social layer. Active prayer-triage + miracles + raids = engaged sessions, not AFK. 'Tap to smite' = simpler than any battler. Live-ops discipline = the actual job (weekly omens, seasonal pantheons).

**The honest caveats (no victory lap without them):**
- It will NOT be a TikTok phenomenon. Discovery comes from the retention algorithm, search ("god game", "idle"), and friend invites — slower fuse, 3–6 months to find out if it works.
- It lives or dies on live-ops discipline. Ship it and neglect it and it's simulator slop by day 60. The plan must include 6 months of weekly events BEFORE launch.
- The god fantasy must be playtested with actual 10–13-year-olds. If 'answering prayers' doesn't delight them in 15 seconds, redesign the hook, not the marketing.

**The portfolio answer (because one bet is never the plan):** Build **Pocket Pantheon** first (the foolproof base hit). If it lands and funds a team, build **Rewind Raiders** second (the moonshot — highest ceiling, needs the team Pantheon pays for). **The Last Lighthouse** is the backup if playtesting kills the god fantasy (cheapest horror pivot). Kill the other 15 with the numbers above and don't look back.
